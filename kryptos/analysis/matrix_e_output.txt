2. Verdict
   Under any cipher whose per-position permutation has no fixed point,
   P(observe >=1 self-map in 24 crib letters) = 0 exactly.
   We observe 2.  => Enigma (all wheel/reflector/plugboard settings, all
   models incl. M3, M4, Zahlwerk, Abwehr), Typex, Porta and Playfair are
   ruled out with certainty, independently of key, rotor wiring, ring
   settings, plugboard and stepping.  No search is required or meaningful.

   Conversely the observation is entirely unremarkable for an UNRESTRICTED
   cipher: expected self-maps = 24/26 = 0.923, P(>=2) ~ 0.236 (Poisson).
   So the self-maps neither support nor weaken any non-reflector hypothesis.

4. Rotor machines WITHOUT a reflector are not excluded -- and not testable
   M-209 / C-38 (the US Army converter Scheidt's generation trained on) is a
   Beaufort machine, C = (k - P) mod 26, so self-maps are allowed and the two
   observed self-maps are no objection.  But its keystream is generated by 6
   pin wheels of periods 26,25,23,21,19,17 (lcm = 101,405,850), so over 97
   letters it is effectively aperiodic: no repeat constraint exists at all.
   key entropy:   pins 131 bits + lugs 120 bits + wheel offsets 27 bits = 278 bits
   crib evidence: 24 letters x log2(26) = 113 bits
   278 >> 113, so consistent M-209 settings are guaranteed to exist by pure
   counting (~2^165 of them) and finding one would prove nothing.
   The same argument applies to SIGABA (15 rotors, no reflector, ~2^95 key)
   and to any one-time-pad-like keystream.  These families are UNFALSIFIABLE
   from the cribs; they can only ever be attacked through the plaintext.
   That is the honest boundary of what crib-consistency testing can do.
