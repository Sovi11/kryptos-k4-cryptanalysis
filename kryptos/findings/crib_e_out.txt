==============================================================================

==============================================================================
E1.  THE TWO READINGS COINCIDE FOR EVERY POSITION-PRESERVING CIPHER
==============================================================================
Claim: if the cipher maps plaintext letter i to ciphertext letter i
(any letterwise rule, any per-position key, any alphabets), then
   'the ciphertext at 64..69 decrypts to BERLIN'   (CT-reading)
and
   'the plaintext at 64..69 is BERLIN'             (PT-reading)
are the same sentence, so task (e) cannot separate them.

Demonstration: build random position-keyed ciphers (a fresh random key
value at every one of the 97 positions, all 12 rule/alphabet combos),
and check the two readings always pick out the identical 24 (ct, pt)
pairs.
  identical in 240 / 240 trials  [PASS]

  So (e) has teeth only when a stage moves letters -- a transposition,
  a fractionation, or a variable-length encoding.  findings/twocipher.md
  enumerates the four (stage order x reading) cases over a 1.17M-entry
  transposition catalogue plus 2.45M compositions and finds nothing;
  the sections below attack the readings from the placement side
  instead, which does not need a transposition catalogue at all.

==============================================================================
E2.  VALIDATION: PLANTED CIPHERS MUST BE RECOVERED

disagreeing: the quoted numbers index the plaintext, and plaintext and

E5.  REVERSED READINGS
==============================================================================
K3 is a route transposition read right-to-left, so 'which end do you
count from' is a live question for this designer.  Two variants:
  R1  the quoted 1-indexed positions counted from the END of K4
      (crib word at 97-hi+1 .. 97-lo+1, word left-to-right);
  R2  the same, with the crib word itself reversed, i.e. the plaintext
      is written backwards under the ciphertext.

==============================================================================
E6.  HOW MUCH DOES EACH READING ACTUALLY CONSTRAIN?
==============================================================================
S = letterwise substitution whose key may vary with position;
T = transposition.  Constraint counts are for a period-p key; c is the
number of independent equalities the 24 crib letters impose, and a
random key passes with probability 26^-c.

  CT-reading, T then S   the 24 (ct, pt) pairs are exactly those of a
                         one-stage cipher; T is invisible to the test.
                         c by period:
    p2:22  p3:21  p4:20  p5:19  p6:18  p7:17  p8:16  p9:15  p10:14  
    p11:13  p12:12  p13:11  p14:11  p15:9  p16:8  p17:7  p18:7  p19:9  
    p20:11  p21:11  p22:9  p23:7  p24:5  p25:3  p26:1  

  CT-reading, S then T   same 24 key VALUES, but sitting at the
                         relocated positions T^-1[c]; testable only once
                         T is enumerated.  Covered exhaustively in
                         findings/twocipher.md (0 survivors).

  PT-reading, S then T   key stream fully determined at the 24 known
                         PLAINTEXT positions for each candidate T; no
                         periodicity needed.  Covered: twocipher.md
                         two2_a/two2_b/two2_d (0 survivors).

  PT-reading, T then S   the 24 key values are known but their POSITIONS
                         depend on T.  For an un-enumerated T the cribs
                         impose c = 0 at every period -- they cannot
                         test it at all.  This reading is in exactly the
                         same evidential position as periods >= 27:
                         crib-free, decidable only by whether the output
                         reads as English.

  Bottom line for (e): the reading question changes nothing for any
  position-preserving cipher (E1), is closed by twocipher.md for two of
  the three transposition cases, and in the fourth case removes the
  cribs from play entirely rather than relocating them.  There is no
  version of (e) in which the standard 24 pairs are simply shifted to
  some other 24 contiguous pairs -- E3, E4 and E5 test those directly
  and find nothing above chance.
