--- 4. Likelihood ratios: what does 'all 26 present' buy you? -------
  P(all 26 | uniform-random / OTP / long key) = 0.5425
  P(all 26 | English (plaintext) ) =    0.00005   LR vs uniform = 12054.7
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=1        ) =    0.00003   LR vs uniform = 21698.5
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=2        ) =    0.02145   LR vs uniform =    25.3
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=3        ) =    0.09109   LR vs uniform =     6.0
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=5        ) =    0.21905   LR vs uniform =     2.5
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=8        ) =    0.33066   LR vs uniform =     1.6
  P(all 26 | Vigenere L=12       ) =    0.40146   LR vs uniform =     1.4

  Reading: the observation 'K4 contains all 26 letters' is ~2x more likely
  under a flat source than under a short-key Vigenere, and ~18x more likely
  than under raw English.  Two-to-eighteen-fold likelihood ratios on a single
  binary observation are weak evidence (a Bayes factor of 2 moves a 50% prior
  to 67%).  The honest statement: coverage tells you K4 is not raw English and
  not a one-alphabet cipher, which the IoC already told you far more loudly,
  and it tells you nothing else.

--- 5. Which letters are rare in K4 ---------------------------------
  letters appearing exactly once: M Y (2 of 26)
  expected number of singletons under uniform random = 2.25
  most frequent: Kx8, Ux6, Sx6, Tx6, Ox5, Bx5
  chi-square vs uniform (df 25) = 19.1  [95th pct 37.7, 99th 44.3]
  chi-square vs English (df 25) = 571.0
  => K4's letter distribution is statistically indistinguishable from flat,
     and decisively NOT English.
