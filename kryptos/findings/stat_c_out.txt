--- 5. The positional folklore, corrected for multiple testing ------
  Observed positions [19, 26, 33, 43, 47, 68].  Residues mod 7: [5, 5, 5, 1, 5, 5] -- five of six are 5 (mod 7).
  Naive p for that alone: P(>=5 of 6 uniform positions share a residue mod 7)
    = 0.00220   (7 residues x binomial tail)
  But modulus 7 was chosen AFTER seeing a lag-7 autocorrelation bump.
  Correct null: max over moduli 2..48 of the largest residue class,
  for 6 positions drawn uniformly without replacement from 1..96.
    1,000,000 trials: P(some modulus 2..48 collects >= 5 of 6) = 0.2452
                  P(some modulus collects all 6)          = 0.0295
  => the mod-7 pattern is NOT significant. It is what you always find when
     you let yourself pick the modulus after the fact.

  Second claim: 'doubles cluster in the last two columns of a 7x14 grid'.
    columns (0-indexed, width 7, 1-indexed positions): [4, 4, 4, 0, 4, 4]
    -- five in column 4, one in column 0; not the last two columns at all
    under this indexing. The claim is indexing-dependent, and the corrected
    p-value above covers every indexing.
